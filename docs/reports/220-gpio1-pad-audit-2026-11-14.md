# 220 — The GPIO1/PAD audit: a real DTS defect (GPMC alternate functions driving the pads), and it is not the WL1283 blocker either

Date: 2026-11-14 (session 49, Linux-era 43).
Bench: two experiment images flashed and booted, then reverted.  No NVRAM,
MCT or RPMB write.

## 0. Headline

Prompted by the operator's two suggestions — re-check the **Linux-vs-OMAP GPIO
numbering** class of defect (trap 185) against the WL1283 work, and do a **full
RE of every QNX binary in the radio's boot path** — this audit found a **real
board-DTS defect that no previous session had recorded**, and then showed that
it is *not* what keeps the card down.

The defect: **GPIO1_0, GPIO1_4 and GPIO1_18 (pads `0x4A100050`, `0x4A100058`,
`0x4A100074`) were left at mode 0**, so instead of a GPIO they drove their
alternate function — `gpmc_ad0`/`gpmc_ad1`, `gpmc_ad12`/`gpmc_ad13` and
`gpmc_ncs0`/`gpmc_ncs1`.  A board with no GPMC device had its GPMC bus lines
actively driven by pad logic, and gpiolib could not control them at all (trap 21:
a GPIO write on an unmuxed pad is a silent no-op).  GPIO1_31 was not muxed
either, and its input buffer was off, which is why trap 134 had to enable the
buffer temporarily just to read it.

Driving them as the reference does (mode 3, pull-down, output low) is **positive
for correctness** — GPIO1's direction word now matches the reference line for
line, bar GPIO1_4, which our DTS gives to the display bridge — and **negative
for the WL1283**: `mmc4: Failed to initialize a non-removable card` with
`GPIO2_DATAIN` bit 21 still 0.  **The fix is kept** (it is a genuine board
defect, independent of the radio); the unit's image `0x0114FFD3` carries it.

## 1. The audit the operator asked for (trap 185's open item)

Trap 185 ended with "**Also audit the WL1283 work:** any experiment that used
sysfs/global gpio numbers rather than DT/OF phandles should be re-checked" and
that audit had never been recorded.  It is now: **the WL1283 work never used a
sysfs global GPIO number.**  Every pin was either driven through a DT
`gpio-hog`/`gpio` phandle (`&gpio2 22`, `&gpio4 31`, …) or read at its absolute
bank address (`0x48055190`/`0x48055194` for `GPIO2` data, `0x4A310134` for
`GPIO1` OE).  So the specific trap-185 error — off-by-N because `/sys/class/gpio`
bases are assigned in probe order — is **absent** from this work.

What the audit *did* surface is the pad half of the same class: a line can be
"the right GPIO" and still not be driven, because its pad is in another mux
mode.  Comparing the pads of every line the reference drives turned up the
three pads above.

## 2. What the reference does, and what we did

Reference (report 218's live capture at `ti0`-up, `GPIO1_OE = 0x6FFB1FAA`): the
outputs are bits `[0,2,4,6,13,14,15,18,28,31]`, of which **0, 4, 18, 28, 31 are
driven low** (15 is low only because no audio is playing).

| pad | line | reference | ours (before) | ours (experiment) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0x050` | gpio_32/33 = GPIO1_0/1 | `010B/010B` (mux 3, pull down) | `0118/0118` **mux 0 = gpmc_ad0/1** | `0118/0103` |
| `0x058` | gpio_36/37 = GPIO1_4/5 | `0103/0003` (mux 3) | `011B/0108` — low half mux 3 (**GPIO2_4**, bridge_pins), high half mux 0 | unchanged (conflict) |
| `0x074` | gpio_50/51 = GPIO1_18/19 | `0103/0103` (mux 3) | `0118/011F` **mux 0/7 = gpmc_ncs0** | `011F/0103` |
| `0x088` | gpio_60/61 = GPIO1_28/29 | `0003/0003` (mux 3) | `0003/0003` | unchanged |
| `0x4A31E058` | gpio_wk31 = GPIO1_31 | `000B/0003` (mux 3) | `0000/0000` (input buffer off) | `0108/0003` |

The experiment (`0x0114FFD3`, kernel #51) put `0x050`/`0x074` in mode 3 with
pull-downs and added five `output-low` hogs.  Result:

* `GPIO1_OE = 0x6FFB1FBA`, i.e. outputs `[0,2,6,13,14,15,18,28,31]` — the
  reference's set **minus GPIO1_4** (which the DTS gives to `bridge_pins`, so
  the pinctrl request for `gpio1-en-pins` is refused with
  `pin PIN36 already requested by 48055000.gpio`).
* `GPIO1_DATAOUT = 0x0000E044` — **identical to the reference's** (bits 2, 6,
  13, 14, 15 high, the rest low).
* `GPIO2_DATAIN` bit 21 **still 0**, `mmc4: Failed to initialize a
  non-removable card`.

So the five reference-only outputs are now driven and low, as on QNX, and the
card is still silent.

## 3. The other half of the audit: the boot-path RE

A full static audit of the QNX boot path was run in subagents (report 219).  Its
useful results:

* **`startup-playbook` makes 144 MMIO stores**, a complete inventory of which is
  in report 219 §Q1.  It *does* enable `CM_L4PER_MMCSD5_CLKCTRL`
  (`0x4A009560 = 2`), which report 217's replay already covered, and it never
  writes `PRM L3INIT`/`L4PER`, i.e. **`RM_L4PER_MMCSD5_CONTEXT`
  (`0x4A307564`) is untouched by the whole boot path** — so the `= 1` our own
  boot performs is not "the reference's power-on write" but the W1C clear of a
  flag, and the two are compatible.
* **`pmic_twl6030_cfg`** — a boot-image program, loaded at `0x8062b000`, which
  writes 38 TWL6030 registers and which Linux never runs.  It is the one
  genuinely new candidate the RE produced.  **It is refuted by the live
  captures**: 15 of its claimed values differ from what TabletOS itself reads
  afterwards (its writes are later overridden by the regulator resmgr), and of
  the remainder none is a rail the WLAN needs — VAUX1/2/3 are `0x01/0x05/0x05`
  (on, APP) in both OSes, and the *only* on/off-class differences are REGEN1/2,
  which QNX turns **off** and detaches and Linux leaves on.  Report 219 §Q3.
* **REGEN1/REGEN2**: `pmic-winchester-twl6030` writes `0xAD/0xAE/0xAF =
  00/01/00` and `0xB0/0xB1/0xB2 = 00/01/00` on DVT3+ — exactly the difference
  visible against Linux's `07/05/55`.  They are the display/panel rail family
  (trap 135/§3.21), and QNX *disables* them, so they cannot be what powers a
  radio that Linux would otherwise leave off.
* **Correction to report 192 §7** (from the same RE): `0x8C`–`0x8E` is
  **VAUX3**, not `CLK32KG` — that address comes from Linux's TWL6032
  `clk-twl.c` table.  Both OSes have VAUX3's APP field on; only group membership
  differs.  The 32 kHz lead stays closed.

## 4. Verdict

* The **pad-mux defect is real and now fixed in the tree**: three pads that no
  session had audited were driving GPMC alternate functions on a board with no
  GPMC device, and a fourth (GPIO1_31) was unmuxed with its input buffer off.
  That is worth keeping regardless of the WL1283 — it is the trap-185 class
  applied to pads rather than to numbers.
* The **WL1283 is unchanged by it**, and the boot-path RE adds no live
  candidate: the only new writer (`pmic_twl6030_cfg`) is refuted by the
  cross-OS captures.  Combined with reports 217/218, the readable and
  reconstructible surface is now exhausted from three directions: the driver's
  own writes, the whole boot path's writes, and the live register state of both
  OSes.

## 5. Traps

* **228.** **A GPIO that is "the right line" can still be undriven: check the pad
  mux, not just the number.**  `0x4A100050`/`0x4A100074` were at mode 0, so pads
  `gpmc_ad0`/`gpmc_ad1` and `gpmc_ncs0` were actively driven by pad logic while
  the GPIO could not touch them (trap 21) — a board defect invisible to every
  previous GPIO comparison because those compared `OE`/`DATAOUT`, not the pads
  of *unclaimed* lines.  This is trap 185's class in its pad form.
* **229.** **A pinctrl group cannot re-mux a pad another group owns**; the kernel
  refuses the second claim (`pin PIN36 already requested by 48055000.gpio`).
  `0x4A100058`'s low half is `gpio_36` = **GPIO2_4**, claimed by `bridge_pins`,
  so the reference's GPIO1_4 low state cannot be reproduced without moving that
  pad's ownership deliberately.  Read the overlap before writing the group.
* **230.** **`pinctrl-single` offsets in `&omap4_pmx_wkup` are relative to
  `0x4A31E040`, and the block is only `0x38` long** (`mux offset out of range:
  0x58 (0x38)`).  Pad `0x4A31E058` is offset **`0x18`** there, not `0x58` — the
  same address convention as `&omap4_pmx_core`, where it *is* `0x058`.
